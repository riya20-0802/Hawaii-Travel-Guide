Hawaii Travel Guide

Best Time to Visit

The best seasons to visit Hawaii are spring and autumn, specifically during the months of April-May and September-October. Visiting during these months gives you the perfect combinations of great weather, fewer crowds, and cheaper prices for flights and hotels!

Weather differs between islands, and even between locations, so be sure to check the forecast before planning outdoor activites, so you can pack accordingly!

What to Pack

Some useful items to bring on the trip to Hawaii include:

  • Lighweight clothing
  • Comfortable Walking Shoes
  • Swimwear and a Beach Towel
  • Sunscreen, Sunglasses, and a Hat
  • A reusable water bottle
  • A light rain jacket
  • A warm jacket for higher elevations

Emergency & Health Safety

While Hawaii is a safe and welcoming destination, it is always important to stay prepared for unexpected situations. Keep the universal emergency number (911) in mind for police, fire, or medical assistance.

  • Familiarize yourself with local urgent care clinics and hospitals near where you are staying.
  • Drink plenty of water throughout the day to avoid dehydration under the strong tropical sun.
  • Watch out for local ocean hazards like sharp coral, strong rip currents, and high surf advisories posted at beaches.
  • Keep a basic first-aid kit in your rental car or day bag for minor scrapes and cuts from hiking or exploring.

Inter-Island Travel

If you plan to visit more than one island during your trip, you will need to book a short inter-island flight. Airlines like Hawaiian Airlines and Southwest Airlines operate quick daily flights that usually take between 30 to 50 minutes.

When booking, try to pack light or check your airline's baggage fees, as traveling between islands requires going through airport security each time. Booking these flights well in advance alongside your main accommodations will save you time and money!

Transportation

Transportation options vary significantly across Hawaii depending on which island you visit, making it essential to find reliable transportation where you are staying!

On neighbor islands like Maui, Kauai, and the Big Island - where top sights are spread far apart and public transport options are limited - renting a car is the best way to travel, particularly when exploring areas outside major towns. Just be sure to check rental requirements and road conditions before driving!

However, if you are staying exclusively in Oahu, you can easily skip the rental car to save money by taking advantage of walkable neighborhoods like Waikiki, relying on Honolulu's excellent public bus system (TheBus), using rideshares like Uber or Lyft, or taking guided tours to visit major attractions without driving.

Hawaiian Culture and Traditions

Hawaii has a rich Native Hawaiian history and culture. Visitors should take the time to learn about local traditions and the importance of the land and ocean to Hawaiian communities.

Hawaiian culture and traditions are built on deep respect for family ('ohana), nature, and community. This unique heritage is kept alive through the spirit of Aloha-a philosophy of living with love and compassion- as well as sacred acts like Hula storytelling and the gifting of flower Leis to show honor and affection.

Here are some general rules to keep in mind as you explore the islands:

  • Respect sacred and culturally significant places.
  • Follow signs and instructions at cultural sites.
  • Respect local communities and their traditions.
  • Do not touch, chase, or feed wild animals.
  • Leave natural areas clean and undamaged.

Travel Tips

  1. Check the weather before outdoor activites.
  2. Book popular tours and required reservations in advance.
  3. Follow beach and ocean safety warnings.
  4. Check current travel and entry requirements.
  5. Respect the environment and local communities.

Reef-Safe & Eco-Friendly Travel

Protecting Hawaii’s delicate marine ecosystems is essential for keeping the ocean vibrant for future generations. One of the easiest ways travelers can help is by choosing the right sun protection.

State law in Hawaii bans the sale and use of sunscreens containing chemical blockers like oxybenzone and octinoxate, which are known to damage coral reefs and bleach marine life. Always make sure your sunscreen is labeled "Reef-Safe" and uses non-nano mineral ingredients like zinc oxide or titanium dioxide instead.

  • Use reef-safe mineral sunscreen to protect coral reefs and marine wildlife.
  • Pack reusable items like shopping bags, water bottles, and utensils to reduce plastic waste on the islands.
  • Maintain a safe distance from sea turtles (honu), monk seals, and dolphins—never touch or crowd them.
  • Participate in beach cleanups or leave every beach cleaner than you found it.
