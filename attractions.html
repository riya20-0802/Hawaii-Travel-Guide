Must Visit Attractions in Hawaii

Hawaii has many incredible places to explore, from scenice coastal roads to volcanic landscapes and unforgettable ocean expereinces!

1. Road to Hana

The Road to Hana is a famous scenic drive along the coast of Maui, Hawaii, known for its stunning natural beauty. The route winds along the coast and passes through tropical rainforests, flowing waterfalls, small bridges, and dramatic ocean viewpoints.

Because the road features hundreds of sharp curves and narrow, one-lane sections (including many one-lane bridges), travelers should allow plenty of time for the drive, follow local road safety rules, and focus on the journey rather than rushing to the final town of Hana.

Top Highlights and Stops

Stunning Coastal Views and Beaches

Waiʻānapanapa State Park: This is the ultimate crown jewel of the drive! It features Paʻiloa Beach, a striking volcanic black sand cove framed by brilliant blue water and bright green jungle, plus sea caves, rock arches, and a blowhole.


Crucial Tip: Non-Hawaii residents must book a timed reservation online in advance; same-day entries are not permitted.

Keanae Lookout:An easy stop to stretch your legs on an old lava peninsula while watching massive ocean waves crash against dark, jagged volcanic rock cliffs.

Kaihalulu Beach:A hidden pocket beach in Hana town featuring deep red-to-maroon sand.


Safety Note: Skip this if you don't have sturdy shoes or are traveling with small children, as the trail runs along a steep, slippery cliffside.

Image Source: This Week Hawaii

2. Nā Pali Coast State Wilderness Park

Nā Pali Coast State Wilderness Park is a beautiful, wild park located on the island of Kaua'i, Hawaii, famous for its tall green cliffs that drop straight down into the Pacific Ocean, deep valleys, and rugged coastline. Because there are no roads here, visitors cannot drive to the park; intead, you can experience the area through permitted hiking trails, boat tours, helicopter flights, or scenice viewpoints. Filled with hidden waterfalls and old Hawaiian history, the region is also home to native plants and wildlife, so visitors should respect the environment and follow park rules while exploring.

Famous Trails and Waterfalls

Kalalau Trail is the legendary 11-mile one-way trail that crosses five valleys. It offers unmatched, cliffside ocean views but is very steep and tough.


Hanakapiai Falls is a stunning 300-foot waterfall tucked deep inside the rainforest. You can reach it by hiking a popular 4-mile round-trip detour from the main trail.


Kalalau Beach is the beautiful, isolated white-sand beach right at the very end of the Kalalau Trail. It is a famous spot for permitted wilderness camping.


Honopū Valley features a famous, dramatic rock arch arching over an isolated beach. It is so hidden that it can only be seen from the air or water!

Image source: HawaiiGuide.com

3. Hawai'i Volcanoes National Park

Located on Hawaiʻi Island (the Big Island), Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park protects important volcanic landscapes and is home to two of the world's most active volcanoes, Kīlauea and Mauna Loa.

It is a massive, dynamic park where visitors can walk through lava tubes, hike across giant volcanic craters, and see volcanic steam rising straight from the earth. The park holds immense cultural significance in Native Hawaiian tradition as the sacred home of the volcano goddes, Pele. Because conditions continuously change with active eruptions, travelers should always check official park updates and stay in permitted areas.

Top Must-See Highlights

Halemaʻumaʻu Crater Overlooks: The main caldera of Kīlauea. Viewpoints like Uēkahuna or Kīlauea Overlook offer dramatic, expansive views of the crater. During active eruptions, this is the prime gathering spot at night to watch the fiery red glow fill the sky.

Kīlauea Iki Trail: A popular loop hike that descends through a lush rainforest and leads directly onto the hardened floor of a solidified lava lake from a 1959 eruption. As you walk across the vast volcanic crater, you can even see and feel heat rising from steam vents in the ground.

Nāhuku (Thurston Lava Tube): A short, scenic walk through a giant fern forest leading into a massive, lit underground cave formed 500 years ago by a river of molten lava.

Chain of Craters Road: A spectacular 19-mile drive that drops 3,700 feet from the volcano summit down to the Pacific Ocean. The route passes past hardened lava fields from various decades and ends near theHōlei Sea Arch, a dramatic 90-foot rock formation carved out of lava rock by crashing waves.

Image source: HawaiiBeaches.com

4. Polihale State Park

Polihale State Park is a remote, breathtaking wilderness beach park located at the end of a long stretch of golden sand on the western coast of Kauaʻi. To get there, you take a scenic drive past sugar cane fields before navigating a bumpy, unpaved dirt road, which usually requires a 4-wheel-drive (4WD) or high-clearance vehicle. Visiting is an unforgettable adventure where you can expect to see dramatic emerald cliffs of the Nā Pali Coast rising behind you, endless pristine shoreline, and some of the most vibrant, fiery sunsets in the entire Pacific. Because it is a wild and isolated area with no commercial facilities, you just need to bring plenty of drinking water, sunscreen, snacks, and a sturdy vehicle, while keeping in mind that strong ocean currents make swimming dangerous here.

Image source: TripAdvisor

5. Pearl Harbor National Memorial

Located on the island of Oʻahu, the Pearl Harbor National Memorial honors the tragic events of December 7, 1941, which drew the United States into World War II.

It is a deeply moving and historic site where visitors can explore world-class museums, view authentic artifacts, and board a Navy vessel to visit the striking white memorial structure built directly over the sunken hull of the USS Arizona. The memorial holds immense national and international significance as the final resting place for over 900 sailors and marines. Because this is one of the most visited sites in Hawaii, travelers should book their timed ticket reservations online well in advance, follow security guidelines regarding bags, and plan to spend a half-day absorbing the rich history.

Top Must-See Highlights

USS Arizona Memorial Program: The centerpiece experience. It includes a short documentary film in the theater and a boat ride out to the floating memorial spanning the sunken battleship.

Battleship Missouri Memorial: Step aboard the historic "Mighty Mo," the final battleship built by the U.S. Navy, where the unconditional surrender ending WWII was formally signed.

Pacific Fleet Submarine Museum: Explore the USS Bowfin, a classic fleet submarine from WWII, and walk through indoor galleries detailing underwater submarine operations.

Image source: National Park Service

Must Visit Attractions in Hawaii

Get ready for adventure! Whether you're gliding through the water with giant manta rays, exploring high-mountain peaks, or ziplining above rainforests, Hawaii has activities for everyone!

1. Manta Ray Night Snorkeling

Manta Ray Night Snorkeling is a world-famous ocean adventure located along the sunny Kona Coast of the Big Island of Hawaii. To experience it, you book a guided evening boat tour that departs from local harbors like Honokohau Marina or Keauhou Bay. Once you arrive at the feeding grounds after sunset, guides deploy large, floating LED light boards into the dark water. These bright lights attract clouds of microscopic plankton, which acts as an all-you-can-eat buffet for the giant resident reef manta rays. When you slip into the water, you can expect to hold onto the floating board and lay completely flat like a log while massive rays—with wingspans up to 16 feet—glide, loop, and do backward somersaults just inches beneath your chest to scoop up the food. For this excursion, the tour company provides your essential gear, including a mask, snorkel, fins, and a thick wetsuit to keep you warm. You just need to wear your swimsuit under your clothes and bring a large towel, a warm sweatshirt or jacket for the chilly boat ride back, and an optional underwater action camera with a secure wrist strap.

Image source: Kona Snorkel Trips

2. Maunakea

Maunakea is a breathtaking, sacred volcano on the Island of Hawaiʻi that stands 13,803 feet above the sea, making it the highest point in the state. To get there, you take a scenic drive up Saddle Road; you can easily take a standard rental car to the Visitor Station at 9,200 feet, but you will need a 4-wheel-drive (4WD) vehicle or a guided tour to head all the way to the peak. Visiting is an absolute bucket-list experience where you can expect to stand above the clouds to watch a glowing, unforgettable sunset before experiencing some of the most vibrant, star-filled night skies on Earth. Because you are so high up, it feels like a cozy winter wonderland, so just remember to bring your favorite warm layers (a thick jacket, pants, and a beanie), closed-toe shoes, and water to sip on while you take in the views.

Image source: South China Morning Post

3. Haleakalā Sunrise Summit

Haleakalā National Park is a mesmerizing, sacred treasure on Maui that centers around a massive, dormant volcanic crater rising 10,023 feet into the sky. To get there, you take a beautifully scenic, winding drive up the Haleakalā Highway (Route 378), which any standard rental car can easily handle all the way to the peak. Visiting is a legendary experience where you can expect to stand above a sea of clouds to witness a surreal, world-famous sunrise or sunset, walk through an otherworldly volcanic landscape that looks like Mars, and spot rare native species like the shimmering silversword plant. Because you are nearly two miles high, the tropical weather transforms into a crisp, high-altitude chill, so you just need to bring warm winter layers (a thick jacket, pants, and a blanket to wrap yourself in), closed-toe shoes for exploring, and a few snacks and water since there are no shops inside the park.

Image source: Google Images

Iconic Food Stops You Don't Want to Miss!

No trip to Hawaii is complete without trying its delicious, Instagram-worthy eats! From plant-based coconut soft serve to colorful, superfood-packed fruit bowls and fresh taro treats, exploring local food spots is an unforgettable part of the island experience!

Banan Soft Serve

Banan is a famous, plant-based sweet shop in Hawaii known for serving dairy-free soft serve made from locally sourced bananas and tropical fruits, presented in reusable coconut shells and papaya halves.

Started by local friends as a food truck, Banan focuses on farm-to-table sustainability by using excess local produce to craft smooth, naturally sweet vegan ice cream. Visitors can customize their coconut bowl creations with colorful toppings like toasted coconut flakes, dark chocolate chips, fresh macadamia nut butter, pineapple chunks, and edible flowers. It is the ultimate refreshing, aesthetic treat to enjoy after a sunny morning at the beach.

Popular Aesthetic Creations

The Amazon Bowl: Dairy-free banana and acai soft serve layered in a half-coconut shell, topped with granola, fresh strawberries, shaved coconut, and local honey.

Papaya Boat: Your choice of tropical soft serve flavors served directly inside a freshly sliced, sweet Hawaiian papaya filled with berries and macadamia nut butter.

Riss & Rosie: A tropical soft serve blend featuring passion fruit (lilikoʻi) and banana, topped with dark chocolate cold dip and crispy toasted coconut.

Image source: Royal Hawaiian Center

Matsumoto Shave Ice

Matsumoto Shave Ice is an iconic historic shop in Haleʻiwa on Oʻahu's North Shore, world-famous for serving colorful, finely shaved tropical ice desserts since 1951.

Unlike traditional snow cones made with coarse crushed ice, Hawaiian shave ice uses razor-thin ice flakes that absorb flavored fruit syrups like a cloud. Visitors line up to create vibrant, camera-ready rainbow combinations poured over delicious hidden layers like sweetened red azuki beans, vanilla ice cream, or chewy mochi balls. To top off the dessert, you can add a "snow cap"—a creamy drizzle of sweetened condensed milk or coconut cream across the top.

Signature Flavor Combinations

The Rainbow: The classic aesthetic combination featuring bright layers of strawberry, banana, and blue Hawaiian syrup over fluffy ice.

Tropical Combination: A refreshing blend of local passion fruit (lilikoʻi), guava, and mango syrups topped with a sweet condensed milk snow cap.

Matsumoto Special: Shave ice served over a base of ice cream and azuki beans, topped with your choice of three syrups and chewy mochi balls.

Image source: Lemon8

The Sunrise Shack

The Sunrise Shack is a bright, yellow-painted surf shack on the North Shore of Oʻahu that has become an Instagram-famous hotspot for colorful, superfood-packed acai bowls and bullet coffee.

Founded by professional surfers, this vibrant little spot focuses on healthy, nourishing ingredients designed to fuel an active day in the ocean. Visitors can sit on bright outdoor surf benches while enjoying thick acai and pitaya blends garnished with aesthetic patterns of fresh strawberries, bananas, chia seeds, and raw macadamia nuts. It is the ultimate go-to morning spot for a healthy, vibrant treat before heading down to the beach.

Popular Menu Favorites

Monkey Bowl: A thick organic acai base blended with peanut butter and banana, topped with granola, coconut flakes, and a drizzle of local honey.

Tropical Pitaya Bowl: A vibrant bright pink dragon fruit base topped with fresh mango, kiwi, chia seeds, and a dollop of almond butter.

Bullet Gold Coffee: Creamy blended coffee infused with MCT oil and grass-fed butter, served in an aesthetic branded cup.

Image source: The Sunrise Shack

Holey Grail Donuts

Holey Grail Donuts is a trendy, eco-friendly donut truck and shop originating in Kauai that specializes in artisanal taro-based donuts freshly fried in organic coconut oil.

The star ingredient is locally grown taro (kalo), a culturally significant plant in Hawaii, which gives the donuts a unique, chewy mochi-like texture and a delicate purple hue. Every batch is fried fresh to order and decorated with inventive, aesthetic toppings like local vanilla bean glaze, edible flowers, crushed honeycomb, and fresh local fruit powders. Served in compostable pink pastel boxes, they offer a modern, Instagram-worthy twist on island sweets.

Signature Flavor Toppings

Original Sin: Freshly fried taro donut glazed with single-origin local vanilla bean and a pinch of pink sea salt.

Dragon Fruit Glaze: A striking bright pink glaze made from fresh dragon fruit, topped with delicate edible flower petals.

The Lilikoi: Finished with a tangy, bright yellow passion fruit glaze and toasted coconut flakes.

Image source: Holey Grail Donuts
